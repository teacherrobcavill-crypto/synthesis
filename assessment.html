Mark Scheme (2 marks): Correct answer is A. Sawtooth waves contain all odd and even harmonics, whereas a sine wave contains only the fundamental frequency (no upper harmonics for the filter to subtract).

Q3. 1960s Modular Patching: Audio vs. CV (Guide 2)

2 marks

On a 1960s Moog Modular, why must you patch ADSR → VCA in addition to the audio chain VCO → VCF → VCA?

Mark Scheme (2 marks): Correct answer is B. Because the VCO oscillates continuously, a Gate-triggered ADSR CV is required to open and close the VCA cleanly per note.

Q5. 1970 Minimoog Model D vs. Modern DAW Plugins (Guide 3)

2 marks

Why did the 1970 Minimoog Model D transform live performance compared with a 1960s Moog Modular, and what key limitation did it still share until modern DAW plugins arrived?

Q7. Audio Signals vs. Control Voltages & 1V/Octave

4 marks

(a) Explain the difference between Audio Signals and Control Voltages (CV) / Gates on a 1960s Moog Modular. (2 marks)

(b) State what 1V/Octave means in Moog's pitch standard, and name the module triggered by a Gate pulse to shape note volume. (2 marks)

Mark Scheme (4 marks): (a) Audio signals carry the audible sound path (VCO → VCF → VCA → Out) (1); Control Voltages / Gates are non-audible automation voltages controlling pitch, filter cutoff, or VCA loudness (1). (b) Every +1.00 V increase doubles oscillator frequency / raises pitch by 1 octave (1); the ADSR Envelope Generator receives the Gate pulse to open/close the VCA (1).

(b) Explain why a producer separates the VCA Envelope from the VCF Envelope when programming a plucky synth bass or slow-blooming synth brass. (2 marks)

(b) Separating VCA and VCF envelopes allows loudness and timbral brightness to evolve independently (1) — e.g. fast Filter Decay + high VCA Sustain creates a bright percussive pluck over a sustained warm bass fundamental, or slow Filter Attack creates a gradual brass bloom (1).
Section C • Extended Response (AO3 / AO4)

3. Hardware Minimoog vs. Modern DAW Plugins (8 marks)

Q9. Extended Evaluation

8 marks

Explain how the 1970 Minimoog Model D built upon 1960s modular synthesis to become a landmark instrument, and evaluate the musical and technical advantages and disadvantages of using a modern DAW software plugin recreation — including expanded features such as polyphony, portamento, arpeggiation, pitch bend range, and unison mode — compared with the original hardware.

Band 4 (7–8 marks) Indicative Content:
  • Minimoog Architecture & Impact (AO3): Hard-wired ("normalled") 3 VCOs → Mixer → 24 dB/oct Ladder VCF → VCA in a portable case with Pitch/Mod wheels (Osc 3 in LO mode doubling as LFO); eliminated live patch cables across Prog Rock (ELP, Yes), Funk bass (Parliament, Stevie Wonder), Synthpop (Kraftwerk), and 1990s G-Funk leads.
  • Hardware Pros & Cons (AO3/AO4): Authentic analogue saturation/drift, zero CPU latency, tactile one-knob-per-function control; vs. monophonic, expensive, heavy, temperature-sensitive tuning drift (requiring warm-up + A-440 ref), and zero preset memory (paper patch sheets, manual sweeps, bouncing to tape).
  • DAW Plugin Pros & Cons (AO3/AO4): Instant total recall, unlimited simultaneous instances, tempo-synced automation lanes, rock-solid tuning, affordable; vs. CPU/latency dependence, OS update risk, less tactile mouse workflow without a MIDI controller, and potentially clinical sound without drift modelling.
  • Expanded Features (AO3/AO4): Polyphony (full chords), Portamento/Glide (expressive pitch slides), Arpeggiation (synced patterns), configurable Pitch Bend Range (±2 st to ±12 st), and Unison Mode (detuned stereo-spread supersaw stacks).
Reference • Key Terminology

4. Basic Synthesis Glossary

TermDefinition
Harmonic RichnessThe density and strength of upper overtones above the fundamental frequency, determining what a subtractive filter can attenuate.
24 dB/oct Ladder FilterRobert Moog's 4-pole transistor ladder low-pass filter that rolls off frequencies above cutoff steeply for warm bass weight.
Resonance (Emphasis)A narrow gain boost centred at the filter cutoff point, adding nasal punch or squelchy acid character.
Control Voltage (CV)A steady or slowly changing electrical voltage automating synth parameters (pitch, filter cutoff, or loudness).
Gate SignalAn on/off voltage (+5V held, 0V released) telling an ADSR envelope when to start Attack and begin Release.
1V/Octave StandardMoog's pitch standard where every +1.00 V increase doubles oscillator frequency (raising pitch by 1 octave).
Normalled Signal PathAn internally hard-wired routing (e.g. VCO → VCF → VCA) requiring no external patch cables.
Portamento / UnisonPortamento slides pitch smoothly between notes; Unison stacks detuned stereo-spread voices for wide supersaw leads.
